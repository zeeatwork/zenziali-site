Conference Paper · PNSQC 2023

Nimble, not Agile

Creating a flexible quality assurance framework for your company's unique SDLC. My first attempt at putting my QA process into words for the public.

Why I wrote it

I think one of the hardest things a professional can do is distill their own process so someone else can repeat it.

I've often worked as the only full-time quality engineer. In a small company, that means a wide range of duties, from everyday manual QA to company process and strategy. There's a second challenge, too. The team might support the idea of QA but not feel the need to improve or iterate on their practices until a major defect shows up.

Small and solo QA teams need a process that works with the team they have, not the idealized, cookie-cutter engineering team that books and documentation use as their example. That's what inspired this paper. I wanted small teams, even ones without decision-making power, to be able to build processes that improve quality, whether buy-in is small or large.

When I wrote it, our testing team was a team of not quite two. The engineers built with Basecamp's Shape Up method: six-week cycles, no sprints, no backlogs, and very little room set aside for testing. Most testing lifecycle models are built to match Scrum or Kanban, so I didn't have a playbook to follow. I also didn't want to change a process my team was happy with. So I asked a different question: how could I add testing to the process we already had, without becoming a bottleneck?

What's inside

  • A time-boxed SWOT analysis of how my team built and tested software.
  • Small process changes, each matched to something the SWOT turned up. They included moving test cases into test management software, taking testing discussions out of Slack, and running ensemble testing sessions with developers.
  • A map of test activities to each phase of our development cycle.
  • Two common team scenarios, The Lone Tester and Team Just Ship It, with a starting framework for each.

That idea still guides how I work. I picked up the same thread at my next role, in Quality Is Glue Work.
