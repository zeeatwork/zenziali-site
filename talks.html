Talks and Workshops

Giving people a reason to talk.

Most of my talks aren't really about me talking. They give people a structure to talk to each other: roles for a quiet breakout room, a timed challenge, a 90-day challenge with accountability partners.

Pacific Northwest Software Quality Conference · 2023 · Conference paper

Nimble, not Agile

Why I made it: I was the only full-time tester on a team that used Shape Up, and there was no testing playbook for it.

What we did: I wrote up the flexible QA framework I built around the process we already had, and presented it on the conference's virtual track.

Women Who Code · 2023 · Kickoff for a 90-day challenge

Creating a Tester's Portfolio

Context: the kickoff for a challenge I ran in the Women Who Code Global QA channel.

What we did: We looked at different kinds of tester portfolios, from personal sites to GitHub, and launched a 90-day challenge: 30 minutes a week, four small activities a week, with accountability partners, co-working sessions, and reviews.

Women Who Code DFW · 2023 · Talk and live practice

Behavioral Interviews: A 20 Minute Primer

Context: an updated, shorter version of my interview talk, given as QA Tech Lead for Women Who Code DFW.

What we did: How to dissect a job description, build a library of stories with the STARR method (Situation, Task, Action, Result, Reflection), and follow up after an interview. We finished with live mock interviews and an elevator pitch breakout.

Ministry of Testing · 2022 · 99 Minute Challenge workshop

Asking Questions Before the Code

Context: a hands-on workshop I facilitated for the Ministry of Testing.

What we did: Teams reviewed an e-commerce wireframe and built a mind map of the questions they'd ask its designer, page by page. Then we shared what we found and reflected on how it differed from how we usually explore a new system.

Junior developers group · 2022 · Virtual talk

The Junior Engineer's Guide to Phone Screens, Behavioral Interviews, and Written Communication

Why I made it: I had a panel behavioral interview as part of a hiring process. The panel liked my interview enough to share the recording with the company's CEO, and a developer on the panel invited me to speak to the junior developers group she ran in the Pacific Northwest about how I prepared.

What we did: How a career changer can prepare: a folder of saved job descriptions, templates, a story library, and follow-up notes that close the loop.

LaunchCode · 2021 · Mentor-led studio sessions

Mob Programming for New Coders

Why I made it: When I checked on breakout rooms, they were often quiet. Students were new to coding and too shy to talk, and nobody had shown them how to work as a group.

What we did: I gave them a framework: one person types, everyone else directs, and a timekeeper rotates the roles every 10 minutes. Then they practiced on short JavaScript exercises.
